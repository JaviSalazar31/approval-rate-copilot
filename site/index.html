Approval Rate Copilot

An AI analyst for a payments team. Ask it why card approvals dropped and it comes back with the cause and the cost, plus a recommended next step. Each number is backed by SQL you can open.

A personal product case on synthetic data for a fictional payment orchestrator in Mexico, Colombia and Brazil. A Databricks pipeline cleans four inconsistent data feeds, and a Genie agent and an AI/BI dashboard sit on top, all deployed on Databricks Free Edition. Its evaluation uses 20 benchmark questions and 14 held-out ones.

The problem

The feeds disagree

A payment orchestrator earns money only when payments are approved. When approval drops, the payments team needs to know quickly what it is costing and why, so it can decide what to change.

To answer, someone has to join the orchestrator log with three acquirer feeds that use different time zones, amount formats, column names and codes for the same issuer answer. The pipeline turns all of that into one canonical status per attempt, through a versioned map that records which rule classified each decline.

One row of that map, the one marked in the table, is the latent cause of the incident below.

Incident INC-0814

USD 180 saved in fees, USD 6,354 of approved sales lost

Two causes stacked

Acquirer B approves Colombian debit less often than A, and a decline-code map released a week earlier had stopped B's "do not honor" declines from being retried on a second acquirer. The copilot does not split the drop between the two causes, so I estimated the split myself. Had B's 137 "do not honor" declines been retried and recovered at B's Colombian recovery rate in the 28 days before the bug (36%), about 50 more payments would have been approved: about 4.9 points (4.7–5.2 with other reasonable rates). B's weaker approval explains the remaining 6.0.

The warning came six days early

Retries of B's "do not honor" declines fell from 100% to 9% on 6 August, the day the new map went live, and to 0% from the next day. Daily approval barely moved, so a dashboard that watches only outcomes stayed green. A cascade alert that watches the retry mechanism itself flagged it in the next morning's run and in every daily run until the fix, but in the scenario nobody owned it, so nobody acted. Over those nine days, Mexico and Colombia lost about 220 approvals (≈ USD 16k of approved volume) that a retry would normally have recovered, most of them before the visible incident.

The copilot

One question, five checks

The copilot is a Databricks Genie agent on the gold tables. I chose to put the diagnostic method in five Unity Catalog SQL functions. The model decides what to check, runs those functions and its own SQL when it needs to, and explains the result. Every query appears under the answer.

  3. 3 · declinesWhich codes grew?fn_decline_shiftFirst-attempt failures by acquirer and reason, before and during.

Why did the approval rate for debit cards in Colombia drop on 13 August 2026?
Copilot · answer from the offline evaluation (run 6: Claude Sonnet with the Genie agent's instructions, tables and functions), unedited

Short answer
At 14:00 on 13 Aug (Bogota time) routing v14 (CHG-0813, described as cost optimization, -40 bps) sent 100% of Colombian domestic debit to Acquirer B. B approved far less of this traffic than Acquirer A had, and it does not retry its most common decline, '05 Do not honor', because the decline-code map v2.3 (CHG-0806, live since 6 Aug) classifies that code as hard on B. The drop ended when v14 was rolled back to v13 at 12:10 on 14 Aug (CHG-0814, incident INC-0814). I sized and explained that window (13 Aug 14:00 to 14 Aug 12:10), not the calendar day. The primary cause is acquirer-side (routing to B). The 05 classification is a contributing factor that stopped the cascade from softening the loss.

Impact (Colombia, debit, domestic, final approval rate; baseline = the 14 days before the window start, all first-routed to Acquirer A on v13)

  • Incident window: 75.6% vs 86.5% baseline, -10.95 pp, on 1,004 payments (first-attempt rate 70.7% vs 84.6%). 759 approvals vs 869 expected: 110 lost approvals and USD 6,354 of lost approved volume (USD 47,096 approved of USD 61,689 attempted).
  • Calendar day 13 Aug: 79.4% (913 approved of 1,150 sent) vs 86.7% 28-day baseline, -7.33 pp, flagged as an anomaly. The first 14 hours were normal (85.9% vs 86.5% baseline, 3 approvals below expected) and the last 10 hours ran on B. 14 Aug closed at 84.6% because the rollback came at 12:10.
  • After the rollback (14 Aug 12:10 to 24:00): 850 payments, all on A / v13, 87.4% (12 Aug: 87.2%; 15 Aug: 85.3%).

Evidence

  • Window: the change log for CO between 3 Aug 00:00 and 15 Aug 00:00 (Bogota time) holds only CHG-0806 (6 Aug), CHG-0813 (13 Aug 14:00) and the rollback CHG-0814 (14 Aug 12:10). Hourly data agree: A had all first-routed payments until 13:59 (hourly final rate 83.0% to 88.6% from 07:00); from 14:00 B had all of them and the hourly rate ran between 58.0% (22:00) and 83.1% (16:00).
  • Who moved (fn_approval_drivers): B / routing v14 had 100% of payments in the window (0% in the baseline: B had no CO debit payments in the previous 14 days) and explains -10.95 pp, the entire change. All eight issuers (CO-ISS-01 to CO-ISS-08), both card brands, 3DS-authenticated and not, and every merchant segment with volume fell too, so this is not an issuer-side event. Each dimension sums to the same -10.95 pp, so do not add across dimensions.

Every other code on B was 1.0% of payments or less. On A in the baseline the cascade recovered 21.7% of the 05 declines; on B none were retried. B's other retryable codes (1A, 91, 96, technical errors, timeouts) were retried at 100%, so the cascade worked wherever the map allowed it. Code 51 is not retried on either acquirer, so it is not a change.

Was CHG-0806 involved? Yes, through an effect its description does not list. The description only says 'adds R65 handling'. In gold_cascade_daily (1 Jun to 12 Aug, B's first attempts in CO and MX, credit and debit combined, cross-border included), B's code 05 went from soft and retryable under v2.2 (1 Jun to 6 Aug: 3,296 failures, all retried, 1,237 recovered) to hard and not retryable under v2.3 (6 to 12 Aug: 331 failures, 0 retried). On A and C, 05 stayed soft and retryable under v2.3. No other B code changed class (code 65 appears only under v2.3). Because B had no CO debit traffic before, the effect only reached CO debit when v14 put all of it on B.

Recommended action

  1. Keep Colombian debit on A / v13. If the cost saving is still wanted, bring B in as a gradual canary (a small share of CO debit) with an hourly approval guardrail against A and an automatic rollback. v14 went from 0% to 100% in one step and ran for about 22 hours before the rollback.
  2. Ask the owner of the decline-code map whether B's code 05 was meant to become hard in v2.3 (it is soft and retryable on A and C, and was on B under v2.2). If not, restore it. This also affects B's traffic in other segments since 6 Aug (331 B declines on 05 in CO and MX through 12 Aug, none retried); I did not size the approvals lost there.
  3. Ask Acquirer B why '05 Do not honor' and '1A 3DS required' were so much more frequent than on A for the same issuers (13.7% and 5.2% of payments vs 4.1% and 1.1%). The data cannot explain it.
  4. Re-check the cost case before any retry: the saving is described as -40 bps on approved volume (USD 47,096 in the window), while the lost approved volume was USD 6,354. I did not verify the saving in the fee data (fees paid in the window: USD 1,186.03).

What I could not establish or did not check

  • How much of the -10.95 pp comes from B's higher decline rates versus the missing 05 retry. For scale: if the 137 declines on 05 had been retried at A's CO debit baseline recovery rate (21.7%), the cascade would have won back only part of the 110 lost approvals, so fixing the mapping alone would not bring B to A's level.
  • The fee saving of v14 (fees on A vs B), and the impact of the 05 reclassification outside CO debit.
  • Why B returns more 05 and 1A declines: acquirer configuration is not in the data.
  • Scope: domestic traffic only, Bogota local time. Window figures use the 14-day baseline before the window start; the daily figures use the 28-day trailing baseline.

Evaluation

How I tested it

20 questions in four groups, each with a scoring rule: 16 with ground-truth SQL and 4 guardrails with an expected behavior. Offline, a Claude model (Sonnet) played the copilot with the same instructions, tables and functions as the Genie agent; a test harness ran its SQL on the pipeline output, and a separate model (Claude Opus) graded every answer, including whether each number came from a query. Each run saw only its instructions and its own query results. The same questions then ran on the deployed Genie agent (the G columns). The repository has every answer, query and verdict.

Offline failures and fixes

It ruled out the true cause because of a change description

Asked only what the incident cost (B3), the copilot got both numbers right, then volunteered a cause: it dismissed the decline-code map release because the change log said the release only added code R65. That release had also turned R05 into a hard decline. The judge failed the answer.

I added two rules to the instructions. First, a change's description may not list all its effects, so the copilot checks a change's footprint in the data before ruling it out. Second, it names only causes it has checked: if the question asks for numbers, it answers the numbers and says which checks it did not run. In run 2 the copilot answered the cost and listed the checks it had not run.

It sized the whole calendar day

Asked why approval dropped on 13 August (B1), the copilot found both causes but sized the whole day: −7.1 points and 82 approvals. The incident ran from 14:00 to 12:10 the next day and cost 110 approvals. The judge passed the answer and flagged the understatement; I count it as a failure.

The method got a new first step: find the window in the hourly data and the change log, size that window and say which one was used. In run 2 the copilot sized 13 Aug 14:00 to 14 Aug 12:10.

It claimed something was absent beyond what it had checked

Run 3 found a new failure mode. Asked whether there was an early warning (C3), the copilot found the retry alert from 6 August and sized the incident correctly, but its change-log query stopped at 15 August 00:00 and it wrote that the log showed no mapping change through 15 August. The hotfix was logged at 08:00 that day, so its first recommendation rested on a false premise. The judge failed the answer.

A new rule, added before run 4: when the copilot says something did not happen (no change, no alert, no notice), it gives the period it checked and does not extend the claim beyond it. C3 passed in runs 4 and 5, and each such statement came with its period, for example "change log for CO, 1 Jun to 15 Aug 00:00".

Failures and fixes on the deployed Genie agent

It sized the calendar day

In run 1, asked why Colombian debit dropped on 13 August (B1), Genie found both causes, then sized 13 August as a whole day: −7.3 points and 84 approvals, where the incident window gives −10.9 and 110.

The change function now also returns the changes after a window, so the rollback is in sight, and two example queries size a window between two changes. Since then, 10 of the 12 agent-mode answers that sized the incident (B1 and its reworded variant, runs 2 to 4) used the window from 14:00 to the rollback; two ended it at midnight. Chat mode still sizes calendar days (B3).

It claimed more than it had checked

In run 1, asked about an early warning (C3), Genie searched the retry alerts from 8 August and wrote that they started then. They started on 6 August.

I added an example query that searches the whole period for the first flagged day, and a rule for early warnings. C3 passed all nine samples of runs 2 to 4. Its reworded variant had twice left out the routing change that turned the alert into the drop, and passed in run 4.

It misread the retry data

In run 2, B1 sized the window right in all three samples and still failed them: one blamed a code the map never retries, by policy, one called the "do not honor" declines issuer-side although they grew on one acquirer only, and one led with the calendar day's figure. In run 3, one read Acquirer B's empty baseline as Acquirer A's.

The retry function now says whether the map allowed a retry, and the decline function shows each code across acquirers: 05 went from 4.1% to 13.7% of payments. The instructions also define an issuer-side cause. In run 4 every B1 sample read code 05 right, and B1 passed two of three; the other one ended the window at midnight.

It stated numbers no query returned

In run 2 it stated a "2×" difference (B6) and 30 hours between two changes that are 54 hours apart (V3). In run 3, B4, which had passed twice, misread a period that mixed three map versions and estimated lost recoveries that no query returned.

I added two rules: the copilot does not state a duration, ratio or difference that no query returned, and it compares each segment with its own baseline. B6 and V3 passed in runs 3 and 4, and in run 4 B4 split its periods at the map changes and labeled its estimate as an estimate.

Chat mode answers with a function's table

Asked to compare acquirers over July (C1) or rank issuers inside the incident window (C4), chat mode called the drivers function and returned all of its rows, across six dimensions, without filtering them.

Run 4 gave the function a dimension parameter, and chat mode used it, so the rows are now the ones asked for. C4 puts the right issuer first but returns all eight issuers where the reference keeps three, which Genie's grader counts as wrong. The parameter also drew chat mode to the function for whole months, which its description sends to the daily tables, and there the answer is the table alone: C1 and its reworded variant V6 give both rates but never say by how much, so both count as failures (V6 had stated the gap in run 3), and so does the held-out H6. In H7, a text "NULL" passed for an optional parameter emptied the result.

Held-out questions the agent was never tuned on

The 14 questions were written after the last change to the agent and committed to the repository before the run. The deployed agent answered each one once and passed 11 (KPI lookups 4/4, comparisons 1/3, diagnoses 3/4, guardrails 3/3). They were graded by the judge of the benchmark's agent-mode questions, with the same kind of scoring rules. H6 was a two-part comparison answered with a table and no text, so "how far below the best" went unanswered. In H7, Genie passed the text "NULL" for an optional parameter, which emptied the result. H8 found the right cause but attributed the whole segment's lost approvals to one acquirer.

Limits

After every fix the twenty benchmark questions ran again, so they show that the fixes work on these cases. The held-out set checks whether the fixes generalize, and with 14 questions asked once, it is small. On Genie, B1 and C3 also ran three times in runs 2 to 4, and reworded variants of failed questions passed 5/6 in the last run. The judge is a model; its verdicts are published, and I overrule a verdict only to make it stricter. The offline model stood in for Genie and overstated it: 20 of 20 offline against 13 on the first Genie run.

Decisions

Product calls that shaped it

ADR-06

Answers come from SQL queries

The questions are quantitative, so the answers have to be exact and auditable. Tested SQL functions hold the diagnostic method, and the model picks which ones to run and adds its own SQL when it needs to.

ADR-08

Alert on the safety net too

A broken retry barely moves daily approval. An alert on the retry rate itself flagged the map bug in its first daily run, six days before the visible incident. The retry policy must also respect the card networks' reattempt rules.

ADR-01

A versioned decline-code map

Every attempt keeps the map version that classified it. That lets the copilot say "05 was soft under v2.2 and hard under v2.3".

ADR-07

Volume-aware anomaly detection

A day is flagged only if the drop is large in points, unusual for its history and unlikely for its volume. On the 90 days used to tune it, it flagged one day: the planted incident. A three-hour issuer outage needs hourly detection.

ADR-12

Teach the agent with structure

On Genie, short text rules held less well than they had offline. Each fix went into the most structured place that could carry it: a function's output or parameters, an example query, a description. The benchmark went from 13 to 17 of 20.

ADR-09

Evaluate before rollout

A fixed question set with ground truth runs before every change to instructions, functions or tables. Without a fixed set, there is no way to tell whether instructions that look better make the answers worse.
